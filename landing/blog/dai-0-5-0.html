Release 24 November 2026 · v0.5.0

Your phone can now share the work.

0.5.0 is about who gets to take part. The computer that was already mining can be joined by the phone in your pocket. A question you ask is work the network does, and pays for. And the answer comes back as something a person can read.

0.4.42 → 0.5.0 The same network, with more people in it
01
A spare phone

Your phone can lend a hand, and you can take it back

The miner on your computer still does the thinking. The phone does not have to become a second miner, and it does not have to keep the AI itself. On the same Wi-Fi you scan a code from the computer, leave the wallet open, and the phone lends spare memory while the computer is working.

Nothing starts until you say so. You can see that it is helping, and you can stop it whenever you want. A phone that sits on a desk all evening is no longer only a phone. It can be part of the work, on your terms.

Spare strength should be a choice you make, and a choice you can see.

02
Fair pay

A question is real work, and someone is paid for it

When you ask the wallet something, that question goes out on the network like any other job. A person running a miner does the work. They are paid for it. The answer is not a private trick that vanishes on one device and leaves the person who did the work with nothing.

You also get a say. Every reply can be rated, not only the special ones. Those judgments are how the network learns what people actually wanted, instead of guessing.

If someone did the work, they should be paid. If you used the answer, you should get a say.

03
A fair chance

Where you live is not a reason to be skipped

The network used to lean toward whoever was closest. That sounds practical. It also quietly left people out. A miner far from the person asking was less likely to be chosen, no matter how ready they were.

0.5.0 stops that. If you can do the work, you can be chosen. The person next door and the person a continent away stand in the same line.

Closeness is not a virtue. Being able to help is.

04
Plain speech

You asked a question. You get an answer.

Too often a tool answers a person with the notes it kept for itself. Prices, names, the weather, a result from another service — 0.5.0 turns that into a sentence you can read.

If you asked for a table, or for the raw data, you still get that. The network only talks that way when you asked it to.

The work can be complicated. The answer should not be.
